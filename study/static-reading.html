Checking claims on a social feed

Read both examples. Identify exactly what the post claims. Consider which source addresses that claim, how the source obtained its information, and whether a second account adds independent evidence. Think about whether you would keep or revise your first judgement.

You can see the Great Wall from the Moon with your naked eye.

NASA — China’s Wall Less Great in View from Space

NASA distinguishes seeing from the Moon from observations in Earth orbit. It describes the Moon visibility story as a longstanding misconception and reports that human-made objects are not visible at that scale. Its discussion of photographs from orbit concerns different viewing conditions.

ESA — Great Wall of China from space

ESA describes Thomas Pesquet photographing the wall from the ISS at roughly 400 km altitude using an 1150 mm lens. The images were assembled into a collage. This account concerns camera photography from orbit, not unaided vision from the Moon. The image credit includes ESA and NASA; this is not a wholly independent replication of NASA’s Moon statement.

What the evidence shows

The NASA account rejects naked-eye visibility from the Moon. The ESA caption describes a long-lens photograph from the International Space Station. A photograph from low Earth orbit does not establish what a person can see unaided from the Moon.

Venus has the hottest planetary surface in our solar system, even though Mercury is closer to the Sun.

NASA — Venus facts

NASA identifies Venus as the hottest planet in our solar system. Its thick atmosphere traps heat. The comparison concerns planetary surface conditions, not temperatures at the centre of the Sun or within planetary cores.

ESA — Venusian geography

ESA describes Venus’s surface as the hottest planetary surface in the solar system. Its explanation focuses on energy trapped by atmospheric gases and particles. This is another institutional explanation, not a new measurement made for this exercise.

What the evidence shows

NASA and ESA describe Venus’s very hot surface and the heat-trapping effect of its atmosphere. Distance from the Sun alone does not determine a planet’s surface temperature. The low like count does not make this supported claim unreliable.
